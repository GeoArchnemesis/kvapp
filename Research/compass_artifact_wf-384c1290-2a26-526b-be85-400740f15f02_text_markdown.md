# Hybrid Nutrition & Wellness Subscriptions: 8-Platform Competitive Teardown (October 2026)

Your 3-tier ladder ($15 AI / $55 Human+AI / $200 All-Inclusive) is well positioned. The market has settled on almost the same three price points: about $15 for app-only or AI (Levels "Build your system" $15/mo, Simple Pro $14.99, Noom Weight annual about $17.42/mo), $45–$75 for human support (WeightWatchers Workshops, Noom month-to-month at $70, the WW Clinic at $74), and about $200 for dedicated 1:1 humans (Future at $199/mo).\[1\]\[2\]\[3\]\[4\] The gap you can own is the $200 tier that bundles a physical gym, labs and a trainer. No global leader offers it, and the Georgian players sell food delivery, not coaching.

## TL;DR
- **Benchmark set:** the 8 platforms below are Noom, Future, ZOE, Levels, WeightWatchers, Simple, BetterMe (founded in Ukraine, the strongest regional analog), and Eat For Fit (Tbilisi/Batumi, the most product-like Georgian analog). They cover lifestyle coaching, AI coaching, human coaching and lab/data-driven wellness.
- **Pricing validation:** your $15 Starter matches Levels' entry price exactly, and Simple's paid tier is within a cent of it. Your $55 Pro undercuts Noom month-to-month ($70) and WW Clinic ($74/mo). Your $200 VIP matches Future ($199/mo). Future's price covers remote training only, and Levels' top tier with labs costs $110.75/mo, so a $200 tier that adds a real gym, labs and a trainer is defensible if you make the bundle concrete.
- **UX pattern to copy:** almost every leader routes its primary CTA into a quiz instead of a pricing page ("Find Your Coach", "Take Our Quiz", "Get your personalized plan", "Get started" → survey). Hero copy is short, outcome-led and emotional. Use a quiz-first funnel, then a transparent 3-card pricing block like Levels'.

## Key Findings

1. **Quiz-first funnels are universal.** Future, Levels, WeightWatchers, Simple, BetterMe and Eat For Fit all point their hero CTA at a multi-step quiz.\[2\]\[3\]\[4\]\[5\]\[6\] Noom routes it to "See if you qualify" / "Start your trial".\[7\] Eat For Fit, a small Tbilisi business, runs a 6-step quiz.\[5\]
2. **Three visible pricing tiers is now the norm among data and wellness players.** Levels shows Build your system / Core / Complete.\[3\] WeightWatchers shows Core / Core+ / Med+.\[4\] Simple has Free / Pro / Premium. Your 3-tier structure fits what users already expect.
3. **Only two of the eight print prices on the homepage: Future and Levels.** Noom, ZOE, Simple and BetterMe hide prices behind the quiz or checkout. Reviewers repeatedly criticize BetterMe and Simple for opaque or variable pricing. Since you are a new brand, showing prices openly builds trust.
4. **The lifestyle tone is winning over the clinical tone.** Simple ("Finally, a health journey that actually feels good. Lasting results. Zero stress."), Future ("Personal training, reimagined")\[2\] and BetterMe ("Body & Mind Wellness Plans for Real Change")\[8\] all sell feelings and consistency.\[2\]\[6\]\[8\] Noom and WeightWatchers have moved heavily into GLP-1 medication. WW's Q2 2026 8-K reports 197 thousand clinical subscribers, up 55.7% year over year, and WW guides clinical revenue to roughly 25–30% of 2026 revenue, up from 16% in 2025. Sacra reports that Noom's GLP-1 programs reached a $100M revenue run-rate within four months of their September 2024 launch. That shift leaves the non-clinical lifestyle space more open.
5. **The lab/data players are getting cheaper and more AI-driven.** In September 2025, ZOE dropped its CGM and blood-fat tests in favour of a single stool test plus AI prediction; the old bundle with blood tests and a CGM cost £299.99. It also cut its UK membership to £9.99/mo, which The Grocer says "works out 60% cheaper than the previous £24.99 price point." Levels now starts at $15/mo for the app alone.\[3\] Labs are becoming an add-on or premium tier, which supports putting labs only in your VIP tier.
6. **Georgia has no direct competitor in hybrid coaching subscriptions.** Local players (Eat For Fit, GoodFood) sell calorie-counted meal-plan delivery with manager-confirmed orders and no coaching layer.\[5\]\[9\] Gyms in Tbilisi charge roughly 160–225 GEL/month.\[10\] Your VIP tier essentially combines a gym membership, meal planning and coaching, which nobody locally sells as one product.

## Recommendations

1. **Hero formula.** Use a short emotional headline, a one-line benefit subhead and a single quiz CTA, with price microcopy under the button (Future's "$50 first month, then $199/month. Cancel within 30 days…" is the best example). Draft: *"Feel better, one habit at a time"* / *"AI guidance, real coaches, and your gym — in one plan built for your life."* / CTA: *"Find your plan"*.
2. **Build a 5–7 step quiz, as Eat For Fit, Future and Levels do.** Steps: goal → activity level → eating style/preferences → schedule → equipment/gym access → "want labs?" → recommended tier. End on a recommended card, not on a paywall.
3. **Copy Levels' pricing block.** Three cards with "Flexible path / Recommended package / Highest-touch package" labels, concrete quantities (e.g., "4 trainer sessions/month, 1 lab panel/quarter, unlimited gym"), and an "Available to add" list for upsells. Show prices in both GEL and USD.
4. **Homepage order to use:** Hero → trust strip (members, rating, partner gym and lab logos) → problem ("Generic advice doesn't fit your life") → how it works (3 steps) → app feature carousel with real UI → coach bios (Future/BetterMe style) → outcomes and testimonials with tenure badges → 3-tier pricing → FAQ → final CTA.
5. **Cheap growth tools:** free BMI/TDEE/macro calculators (Simple), a mascot or streak mechanic (Simple's Blinky, ZOE's plant counter), and Telegram/WhatsApp ordering and chat (the norm locally at GoodFood).
6. **Stay non-clinical.** Noom and WW are going medical with GLP-1s; WW's clinical subscribers rose 55.7% year over year to 197 thousand in Q2 2026. Keep labs framed as "insights" (as ZOE and Levels do with their wellness-only disclaimers), not diagnosis.

## Caveats
- Hero copy reflects the live fetches made on October 6, 2026. Noom visibly A/B tests its hero, and WW and ZOE run rotating promo pages, so wording can change week to week.
- I could not fetch the ZOE root homepage. Its hero is taken from its main conversion pages (buymembership, how-it-works).
- Simple, BetterMe and ZOE (US) prices come from third-party reviews, not the brands' own pages, and vary by region, platform (web vs. App Store) and promotion.
- Eat For Fit and GoodFood prices load dynamically and were not retrievable. The GEL figures for Inebe.ge, NutroApp and gyms come from older or secondary sources and need local verification.
- Outcome stats quoted by brands (Noom "48% more weight", Levels "81% improved HbA1c", Future "98% more consistent") are self-reported or retrospective, not independent trials.
